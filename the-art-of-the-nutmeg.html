The anatomy of the nutmeg.

23 October 2026 | 4 min read

Aurélien Chedjou on Zlatan Ibrahimovic that elicited the famous Zlatan smirk, Adel Taarabt rolling the ball straight through Craig Fagan’s legs in an almost never-seen-before fashion, Philippe Coutinho’s nasty slide through on an onrushing Ersan Gülüm, Luis Suarez on David Luiz before his unnecessary top bin one-on-one with the goalkeeper, and most notably Messi on Milner that gave us the legendary Pep Guardiola reaction.

All of these have one thing in common – the nutmeg. This is perhaps the easiest football skill to execute because it's so simple: you just roll the ball through the opponent's legs, but it is definitely one of the most embarrassing, given the reactions of nutmegged players and fans alike.

A skill so simple that it doesn’t matter what skill level or ability to manipulate the ball a player possesses, but it is still as embarrassing as the Neymar-esque chops and feints that get you off your feet. Imagine for a second Lionel Messi pulling off a nutmeg on you, pretty standard, right? Now, flip it. What if you pulled off a nutmeg on Lionel Messi? Yeah, that is unbelievable and absurd. It can’t happen. But what if, for a moment, you simply played the ball through his legs and ran. That’s it! That’s how easy it could be (potentially) to pull off the most embarrassing skill on the GOAT. Simple and effective.

Trying to replay multiple scenarios of a nutmeg on a player, I still can’t quite wrap my head around what exactly makes it so damning to be nutmegged. Is it the unexpected nature? The fact that a player beats his player in the simplest way he could think of? Or is it because of the optics of being nutmegged?

I think the most interesting explanation is that a nutmeg makes being beaten look almost effortless, as though the defender didn't exist.

A defender can accept being beaten by an elastico, croqueta, and even a couple of stepovers. Those things demonstrate an opponent's superiority. But a nutmeg? A simple act of deception? The attacker has beaten you by exploiting the space between your legs, sometimes with barely any elaborate movement at all.

Perhaps that's the point. Sometimes, the simplest idea is the most effective.
